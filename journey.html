How I ended up building AI for health

When I was ten, I read about a pancreatic cancer vaccine being developed abroad. My father had died of that same cancer, caught too late, on my second birthday, a loss that had shaped me long before I understood why. That day I decided I wanted to spend my life working where health, biology, and technology meet. I didn't have the language for it yet, but it has been my north star ever since.

The path was anything but linear. I wanted to be a genetic engineer, chased the medical entrance exams, fell short, and briefly studied microbiology before realising I couldn't do biology without the math and logic I loved. I landed in computer science on a full scholarship and slowly saw it was the better door into the same room: I could bring biology and medicine into code instead of the other way around. Then machine learning clicked, and I finally had the tools to chase the thing I'd wanted since I was a kid.

The thread hasn't changed since. I've published research, filed a patent application, and kept picking problems where AI meets health. And somewhere along the way I learned to trust myself. I've switched fields with a lot of catching up to do, started in technical support barely knowing what a localhost was, and become the person who built the team's first training program. I don't expect to be the most prepared person in the room on day one. I just know that if I put my head down, I'll get there, usually further than I expected.

What I care about most is trust.

In anything that affects real people, a confident wrong answer is worse than an honest "I don't know."

That's the ethic I build on. I'd rather ship something reliable than something that just looks good on a slide, and it shows up in the work: a no-call option when the genomic evidence is weak, flagging small-sample overfitting instead of publishing a perfect-looking score. Today I'm at Penn, building and evaluating AI products, and heading toward the place I've been walking toward my whole life, where AI and health meet.
